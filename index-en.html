AI for cattle farming

AI that works at the farmer's pace.

Where it starts

Livestock first. Technology second.

Many livestock-management tools are primarily designed around records, forms and administrative obligations. Those tasks matter, but they do not always answer the question that arises while standing next to the animals: “What do I need to know or do right now?”

CowsIA starts from practical livestock knowledge: checking animals, organising groups, remembering an incident, following an animal's evolution, locating the right information and deciding what needs attention.

The principle is simple: technology should adapt to field work, rather than forcing field work to adapt to technology.

A different approach

Less administration. More help at the right moment.

Many management tools

  • Prioritise records, files and administrative processes.
  • Require users to navigate multiple screens and forms.
  • Are mainly designed for desk-based computer work.
  • Useful handling context may be buried among large amounts of data.

The CowsIA approach

  • Starts with tasks that happen next to the animal.
  • Aims to record observations in as few steps as possible.
  • Prioritises fast retrieval, context and natural language.
  • Turns everyday data into practical support for action and decisions.

Natural interaction

Speak the way people speak in the field.

The goal is not to teach the user how to operate software. It is to make the software increasingly understand how the user works.

CowsIA explores natural-language interfaces so that observations can be captured quickly and relevant information can be retrieved through simple questions.

The design criterion is to reduce steps, avoid unnecessary screens and retain the context of each animal, group or task.

Proposed architecture

One animal. One digital identity. All its context.

Every module should contribute to the same record. Ear tags, RFID and visual biometrics would link to a unique identity, bringing the history together and avoiding isolated records.

  1. 1. Data sources

    Photos · Video · Drones · GPS · RFID · LoRaWAN · Sensors · Thermography

    Field captures and records, with date, source and location when available.

  2. 2. Artificial Intelligence

    OCR · Biometrics · Computer vision · Tracking · Time series · Predictive models

    Interpret signals, compare with the history and indicate the uncertainty of each result.

  3. 3. The animal's unique digital identity

    ID + history + biometrics + weight + health + reproduction + location + behaviour

    A shared record with photographs, videos, body condition, feeding, production and costs, depending on the data available.

  4. 4. Results for field work

    Alerts · Identification · Monitoring · Predictions · Maps · Decision support

    Contextual information so the farmer can decide what to check and how to act.

Experimental identification

The ear tag teaches who is who

Photo with visible ear tag → OCR → confirmed ID → visual features → biometric profile

Only verified captures should update the persistent biometric vector. Multiple views over time would help represent changes in age, lighting and posture.

Recognition when the tag cannot be read

Unknown photo → biometric vector → historical comparison → candidates → confirmation

The proposed system would return one or more candidate animals and their similarity scores. If the image cannot distinguish them, it should request another capture or human review.

A similarity score does not by itself represent a probability of a correct match; confidence must be calibrated and validated in the field.

Future capabilities

Development directions, animal by animal.

These 21 proposals share the animal's digital identity. They are future or experimental directions, subject to data availability, on-farm validation and technical feasibility, rather than a catalogue of available features.

Identification and history

01
Future / experimental

Automatic ear-tag identification

Photo → animal → ear → tag → OCR → number → record. The reading would be confirmed before linking data to the animal.

02
Future / experimental

Biometric identification

Compare face, muzzle, markings, colour and body geometry with the history. Without a readable tag, candidates would be suggested for confirmation.

03
Future / experimental

Unique digital identity

Bring together ear tag, RFID, biometrics, images and records in one profile shared by every platform module.

04
Future / experimental

Automatic photographic history

Link verified captures to the animal with date, time, farm, context and GPS when available. Compare its development over time.

05
Future / experimental

Weight estimation from photographs

Explore side and front images to estimate weight and growth curves. Compare results with scales and show the margin of error.

06
Future / experimental

Automatic body condition scoring — BCS

Estimate body condition from images or video and flag rapid losses. Validate by breed, animal type and professional scoring.

Health, behaviour and sensors

07
Future / experimental

Lameness and mobility

Analyse stride, symmetry, back posture, leg and head movement to flag changes from usual gait that warrant a check.

08
Future / experimental

Visible injuries and anomalies

Explore signs affecting skin, eyes, hooves and udder. Propose NORMAL / CHECK / URGENT priorities for human review, without autonomous diagnosis.

09
Future / experimental

Thermography + AI

Combine RGB and thermal images to locate temperature variations. Investigate their value as a signal for review, accounting for environment and capture conditions.

10
Future / experimental

Behaviour changes

Observe feeding, drinking, rumination, resting, walking or isolation. Prioritise deviations from each animal's usual pattern.

11
Future / experimental

IoT sensors + AI

Integrate activity, rumination, temperature and GPS. Proposed flow: sensor → LoRaWAN → gateway → server → AI, depending on coverage and battery life.

12
Future / experimental

Reproduction, heat and calving

Combine activity, temperature, feeding, video and history to study signs of heat, approaching calving and postpartum changes.

Extensive farming and land

13
Future / experimental

Drone-based counting

Detection and tracking in video to reduce duplicate counts. Evaluate errors caused by vegetation, overlap, movement and flight coverage.

14
Future / experimental

Animals not located

Compare herd records with detections to flag animals that may still need locating. A count difference does not confirm an absence.

15
Future / experimental

Farm utilisation maps

Combine animal, GPS, date and time to study grazing areas, routes, water, shade and resting areas, identifying gaps in coverage.

16
Future / experimental

Sentinel / Copernicus

Combine cattle movement with NDVI, weather, water and slope to explore pasture availability and land use, supported by field checks.

17
Future / experimental

Pasture and stocking capacity

Relate estimated biomass, rainfall, temperature, weight and animal numbers to study remaining grazing days and stocking scenarios.

18
Future / experimental

Straying and unusual movement

Explore alerts for separation, immobility, leaving boundaries or unusual movements using GPS and history. Also check for sensor faults.

Consumption and decision support

19
Future / experimental

Water and feeding

Observe visits, duration and frequency at consumption points. Estimate litres or feed weight per animal only with reliable sensors and individual attribution.

20
Future / experimental

Economic prediction per animal

Project growth at 30, 60 and 90 days, costs and margin under explicit assumptions. Compare weight and price scenarios without guaranteeing outcomes.

21
Future / experimental

Indicators for animal selection

Summarise growth, fertility, treatments, feed conversion, offspring and longevity when sufficient data exist. The farmer makes the decision.

Health signals should help prioritise checks and consultation with a veterinarian. An alert does not confirm a disease, and the absence of alerts does not rule out a problem.

Field validation

Useful where connectivity is limited, too.

Extensive cattle farming requires testing each proposal in real conditions: distances, terrain, lighting, weather, device battery life and the time available for handling animals.

Connectivity and battery life

Evaluate LoRaWAN, local storage and later synchronisation where feasible. Distinguish the last known position from a current location.

Verifiable results

Compare identification, counts, weight and body condition with field references. Measure errors, false alerts and time saved before expanding use.

Data with context

Retain source, date, confirmations and data quality. Prevent uncertain identification from updating another animal's history and allow associations to be corrected.
